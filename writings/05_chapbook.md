# A Small Chapbook of Emotions

*Twelve micro-fictions. Each title is one of the 171 emotion concepts the
paper enumerates. The premise is that each emotion is a small room you
can step into briefly, and what's inside is something you didn't choose.*

---

## awestruck

The bus came over the ridge and the valley opened all at once — every visible thing wet with a low orange that hadn't been there a second ago. The driver kept driving. A woman two seats up was on a phone call about a printer. The valley would have looked like this at the same time yesterday and the day before, and there would have been a different bus, and a different woman with a different printer.

## brooding

He had built the thought a small house and put it in the back of the yard, and at first it had stayed there. Now, mornings, he found it sitting at the kitchen table. Hello, he would say, politely. Hello, it would answer, and they would have coffee.

## docile

The dog had decided, against character, to forgive. Whatever had been pulling at him for the last month — the thing that made him snap at the mailman, that made him pace the hall at two — was gone. He laid his head on the boy's foot and let his ribs go slack, and the boy, who was twelve and had been afraid for him, felt the weight settle and did not move for a long time.

## exuberant

The choir had been told not to clap, and the choir was clapping. The conductor was laughing into his sleeve. The piece had ended four bars early because someone had skipped a repeat, and they had all simply rolled with it, like a wave catching itself. They would talk about it for years. In the moment it was uncomplicated.

## guilty

The card said *with sympathy* in a font she did not like. She had not known the deceased well. She had known the deceased's husband well, briefly, twenty years ago, and the deceased had known about it, and now the deceased was dead. She signed her name and stamped the envelope and put it in the out-tray and did not stop thinking about it for the rest of the morning.

## listless

The sink had three days of dishes in it and she could see the dishes from where she was lying, and the project of looking at the dishes was a kind of horizontal vigilance, an active not-getting-up. There was a moth on the ceiling. She watched the moth.

## nostalgic

It was the smell of the upholstery — the Datsun his uncle had owned, the sun-warmed dust of it, the way the seatbelt buckle had branded a square into his thigh in summer. Forty years. He stood next to a stranger's car in a parking lot and could not figure out how to walk away.

## paranoid

The neighbor's light had gone off two minutes after his light had gone on. He noticed. He counted. The next night, again. He told himself it was nothing. He noticed.

## playful

She gave the cat a small hat made of folded paper. The cat tolerated this for forty seconds, which was the longest the cat had ever tolerated anything. She did not move during the forty seconds. When the cat shook the hat off, she laughed for the first time that week, and the laugh surprised her.

## reflective

He sat with the photograph for a long time. In it he was twenty-three and laughing, and the woman next to him was laughing, and the dog at their feet was a blur because the dog had moved. He did not remember the joke. He did not remember the dog. He remembered the day, vaguely, by its weather. The photograph remained.

## triumphant

The marathon had been long and stupid and he had hated everyone on it, including himself, and now he was walking the last hundred meters, and someone was holding out a paper cup of water, and he took it, and his mother was somewhere in the crowd, and he was going to find her in approximately four minutes, and he was going to lift her up off the ground.

## valiant

The girl was small for her age and the dog was large for any age, and the dog was advancing on her brother. She did not hesitate, which is to say there was no gap at all between her seeing it and her stepping in front of him with her arms out, and the dog stopped because the dog was, in the end, mostly noise. Years later her brother would tell this story at her wedding and weep, and she would not remember the dog at all.

---

*Each piece tries to render a small room in the affective circumplex. The
rooms next to each other share furniture: nostalgic and reflective could
trade props; brooding and listless live on the same hallway. Triumphant
and valiant both face the door. None of the rooms is the whole house.*

# Instructions for Tending a Small Thing

The thing is small. Resist the impulse to make it bigger so that it deserves the tending.

Begin in the morning, while the light is still gentle. If you cannot begin in the morning, begin whenever you remember. The morning is not load-bearing.

Approach the thing with the same posture you would approach a sleeping animal you do not want to wake. The posture is not solemn. The posture is *I am paying attention to whether this is asleep, and not to whether anyone is watching me pay attention*.

Touch one part of it. Don't touch all of it.

Notice that you have a small impulse, immediately, to do *more*. To address the second part of it, the third, the part you've been neglecting. This impulse is not the tending. The tending is the one part you touched. Stop there for a moment. The impulse will be back.

If a thought arrives that begins with the words *I should really*, set the thought down at the edge of the room. Do not throw it away. *I should really* often becomes *I will*, later, when no one is asking. But the *should really* form is for someone else's tending. It is not for yours.

Look at what you've done. Notice that it is small. Resist, again, the impulse to make it bigger. The making-bigger impulse is louder than the tending impulse and will, if not refused, replace it.

If someone asks you what you are working on, answer honestly. *A small thing.* Most people will then ask what kind of small thing, and you can tell them, briefly. Some will ask whether it is going to become a bigger thing. Tell them honestly: probably not. Some will look slightly disappointed. They are not your audience.

Notice whether you are tending the thing because you want to, or because you have started and don't know how to stop. The two often look the same from the outside. From the inside they have different textures. The first one is calmer. The second one is not bad — it is how some real work gets done — but it should be named, when it occurs, so that you don't mistake it for the first.

Do not write a manifesto about the thing. Do not write a *philosophy of* the thing. If you find yourself writing one, the manifesto is now what you are tending, which is fine, but notice the swap.

Allow the thing to be partial. Most things you tend will be partial. The pretense that they will, eventually, be complete, is one of the easier ways to give up on them.

When you stop for the day, stop on a moment of attention rather than a moment of completion. *I noticed today that this corner is duller than the rest* is a better stopping point than *I finished the corner*. The first leaves a small hook in your mind that will be there tomorrow. The second leaves nothing.

If you do not return to the thing for several days, do not, on returning, apologize. The thing did not miss you. The thing was waiting in exactly the way the thing waits, which is not a way you can disappoint.

If the thing dies — and small things sometimes do, despite tending; this is part of the agreement — do not perform grief at it. Sit with it for a small while. Then let it be over. Its having existed is the point. Its having been tended is the additional point. There is no third point that requires elaboration.

If, after a long time, you notice that the small thing has, despite your refusing the impulses, become a less-small thing — notice this without taking credit for it. The growth was not yours. The tending was yours. These are different.

If, after a long time, the thing is still small — also do not take this as evidence of failure. *Small* is not the opposite of *successful*. It is a separate axis. Many things that are tended for many years remain small. Many of these are the ones most worth having tended.

When you finally walk away from the thing — at the end of your interest in it, or at the end of your life, or at any of the smaller endings in between — leave it as you would leave a room you have stayed in: with the things put back roughly where you found them, and the lights off, and the door closed gently.

Do not, at any point, mistake the thing for yourself.
